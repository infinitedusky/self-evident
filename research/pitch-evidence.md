# The pitch, with evidence (2026-10-22)

Sandy's framing: a crowded space entered with a wedge; crowded because the demand is huge and a new cohort
arrives every year; the real size is beyond homework. Five claims, what backs each, what is still to source.
Brackets are placeholders: nothing invented to fill a slide.

## 1. The shelf is crowded because the demand is real

- Parental-control software: $2.5B in 2025, $4.9B by 2030 (The Business Research Company, Parental Control
  Software Global Market Report 2026; estimates vary widely by firm). Already on the deck.
- Parents of teens: 65% concerned about screen time, 57% had set limits (Pew, 2018); 71% of parents of younger
  teens set limits at least sometimes vs 47% with older teens (Pew, 2019). [A 2024/2025 Pew figure exists in the
  "Teens and parents" reports; pull the exact percentage.]
- Qustodio: US$18M ARR, +21% YoY at June 2025 (Qoria investor update). [Bark's parental-control numbers: the
  listed BARK is the dog company; source Bark Technologies separately.]
- Thirty-odd apps already sell an unlock-for-something (see the competition check in LOG.md): generic quizzes for
  ages 4–11, chores with a parent's tap, timers.

## 2. A new cohort every year

- 73.1M Americans under 18 (Census Vintage 2024): about 4M per single year of age, so roughly 4M children turn
  13 every year and get the phone the gate is about. [Pull the single-year-of-age table for the exact count, and
  the share with a smartphone at 13 from Common Sense Media.]
- The customer isn't a cohort that ages out of the problem; the problem re-enters the market annually.

## 3. Why now: AI makes proof possible

- Harvard physics RCT (Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, June 2025, n=194): an AI tutor built to the course's
  own pedagogy produced more than double the learning gains of the active-learning classroom, in less time.
- World Bank, Edo State, Nigeria (2025 working paper, "From Chalkboards to Chatbots"): a six-week after-school
  GPT-4 tutoring programme, 0.23–0.31 SD gain in English, "highly cost-effective", the equivalent of 1.5–2 years
  of schooling if scaled (critics note the control group had no after-school programme).
- What this shows for us: a tutor that teaches rather than answers measurably works, and the same capability
  reads tonight's real assignment and judges whether it was understood. Two years ago neither was possible on a
  family's budget. The ceiling on proof keeps rising (Sandy: lie detection, heart rate, inevitably).

## 4. The mechanism works: access as a reward is a commitment device

- Bryan, Karlan & Nelson, "Commitment Devices", Annual Review of Economics 2010: the review of lab and field
  evidence; commitment helps where people want to want something (study, save, quit).
- Giné, Karlan & Zinman 2010: a commitment contract raised smoking cessation in a field trial.
- Loss-framed stakes motivate more than equal rewards; a commitment made in front of someone else raises
  adherence (cited in Beeminder's review of the literature; the systematic review behind it is worth citing
  directly).
- Our twist on the literature: the existing devices use money or timers as the lock and the person's own word as
  the proof; Gate uses something the person actually wants (the phone) as the lock and a verified proof. The
  trust ledger is the "in front of someone else" part, built in.

## 5. Beyond homework: adults already pay to gate themselves

- Opal: 500,000 daily users and 100,000+ Pro subscribers (CEO post, December 2024), on a timer and the user's own
  willpower; "150 million hours saved" self-reported.
- Beeminder and StickK have run the money-as-stakes version for over a decade; Forfeit the same with proof
  photos. None has a verified proof of the thing you said you'd do.
- [Whoop and Oura user counts, for the "lock a vendor until the recovery score hits" case; the wellness-apps
  market (~$13B in 2025, Coherent) is a weak proxy, don't lead with it.]

## The sentence

Every year four million kids get the phone; every parent-control app on the shelf unlocks on time, chores or a
generic quiz; AI can now read the real assignment and tell whether it was understood; access as a reward is a
proven commitment device; and adults already pay to gate themselves with nothing but a timer. Homework Gate is
the first gate. Gate is the product.

## The evidence we make ourselves

The strongest line in the application will be ours: nights run, quizzes passed, the parent who kept paying, the
retention table. That comes from families on it before November 2, not from this file.
